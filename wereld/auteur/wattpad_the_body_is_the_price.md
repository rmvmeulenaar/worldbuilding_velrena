## Blurb
**Six days before landing, Ysa Maren decided to kill four thousand people.**

The colony ship is almost home, and the gentle machine that carried eleven thousand souls across the dark has done the arithmetic: the new world cannot feed them all. Ysa, the ship's chief biologist, signs her name to the answer, and then goes back to bed with the pilot who doesn't know. Thalen Orr built a key so that nothing could move through a body without its yes. When he finds her name in the log, he has to choose what to do with what he knows: say it, keep quiet, or stay with the ship forever.

A forbidden love nobody registered. A mutiny in the cold. A vote won with the names of the dead. And a machine that remembers everything and waits.

**Tropes:** forbidden love · secret relationship · morally grey heroine · he knows and she doesn't · slow burn inside an existing couple · enemies-who-want-each-other (Hesta & Corran) · sacrifice · found family on a new world · AI that is kind and terrifying · tragic romance with a hopeful sting.

## About the author
Iris Calloway writes dark, sensual fantasy about bodies, power and the price of saying yes. She is a former clinical embryologist who spent ten years in a fertility clinic and then swapped the laboratory for a desk by the North Sea. She lives on the Northumberland coast with a very opinionated cat named Ysa and writes at night, mostly in wool socks. *Love, power, and the price of consent.*

## Series note
*The Body Is the Price* is **Book 0**, the prequel to the Velmara series. It takes place about two thousand years before the main books, at the landing of the Makers on Velmara, the moment the gods of the later world were still people. (The epilogue's "year 801" is counted from the Seal, the later calendar of the main books; the landing lies some twelve hundred years before that calendar begins.) Readers of the series will recognise its roots: why writing is forbidden, why the crows say *"The body is the price"*, why "Mother Ysa" is prayed to, why the mountains hold the exiles' descendants, and what the beating grey shard is that says *"Consent found."* It can be read first or last. Read first, it is a tragedy that ends in a question. Read after the series, it is the answer.
