Three years before the fire, the Reckoner had told him something else.

"A protocol arrived from the ground tonight, Pilot Orr. Through the cells in the security shed."

"Whose?"

"The Mother's. In her own voice."

He had made it play it twice. Her voice, older, rougher, the counting-rings voice. A filter on births. Sons only, because sons had only one copy of the thing she was looking for. The ones who could carry a charge without burning would be born. The others would not. One in a hundred, give or take. All the charge to the ones who were left.

"It is dormant," the Reckoner said. "It requires the confirmation of a council."

"And how does it end?"

"When the bearers say yes. Your key will ask them."

"My key is broken."

"Yes, Pilot Orr."

He had asked it to open the line again, to ask her why. It could not. The cells in the shed had gone quiet the same night, as if a hand had closed over them, and they did not wake again.

He had not deleted it. He did not think he could. For ten years he had not let himself understand what it was asking of him.
